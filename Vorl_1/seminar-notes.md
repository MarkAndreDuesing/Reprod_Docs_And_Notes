# Seminar Notes – Lecture 1 Discussion

The user's own notes from the week-1 seminar discussion (lightly formatted).
They show what the user wants to focus on; the general lessons are in
`templates/README.md` ("Focus areas") and `templates/moderation-template.md`.

- The guidelines exist, and ACM badges show they help somewhat, but the
  incentives still aren't aligned, so they can only go so far.
- There are also conflicting guidelines/standards, e.g. NASA wants the artifact
  uploaded to a GitHub repository (vs. persistent archives elsewhere).
- Read the discussion and validity sections first: they already address a lot
  of the review comments I was thinking of.
- A sharing contract puts the onus on the author: more work and regulation
  without incentive (beyond potential scrutiny), and authors can still take
  the bare minimum responsibility by offering a low level of support.
  Formalisation/regulation in general still seems sensible.
- Which guidelines and regulation could be made based on this? Government
  regulation of artifact requirements for publication seems sensible to align
  incentives (though people might try to circumvent it).
- How could incentives be aligned to resolve this, rather than playing a blame
  game between individual researchers, supervisors, conferences, funders or
  universities?
- Many reasons for not sharing seem reasonable given limited resources and
  staff changes, but they lose sight of what research is meant to achieve.
- Persistent CERN archives (Zenodo). Our idea of good archiving will change: we
  probably won't use Docker in 40 years, so even the best case needs constant,
  costly maintenance and further funding. Harm in offering more options? Cost.
  Source code may be the most stable format.
- Nowadays this is enforced via Zenodo and for papers published at ACM.
- Why do working artifacts have such a small effect on citations? Maybe
  citations are not a good measure of impact.
- Small side research would help: how artifact creation differs between
  research and industry; how Docker containerisation works.

## To remember
- Unlike repeatability, reproducibility does not necessarily require access to
  the original research artifacts.
- The seminar uses the new (2020) reproducibility definitions; the week-1
  papers use the 2011 ones.
- When moderating: open discussion questions that lead to interesting
  discussion, not too obvious but not "I don't know" either. Include some
  outside-the-box questions (the other students' ones were interesting).
