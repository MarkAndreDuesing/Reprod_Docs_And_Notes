# Winter, Timperley, Hermann, Cito, Bell, Hilton & Beyer (2022): Condensed Notes

_Page numbers are printed proceedings pages (145–156 = PDF pages 1–12)._
_Stefan Winter (first author) is the seminar lecturer; Dirk Beyer heads SoSy-Lab. Hermann and
Timperley are the first authors of the two week-2 papers._

## Core claims
- After 10 years of AE in SE and PL there is still little evidence that evaluated artifacts live up to the expectations, or that the overhead is justified (p. 145).
- Articles with artifacts **do not get more citations** (Finding 1, p. 149).
- **Available badges** go with better availability, because they require archival hosting (Finding 2, p. 152). This is the only result the authors read causally (§9, p. 154).
- Evaluated artifacts show slightly more GitHub activity (Finding 3), unclear reuse (Finding 4), different documentation styles, and many lack licences (Finding 5).

## Key definitions
- **Artifact:** ACM Task Force definition, "a digital object that was either created by the authors to be used as part of the study or generated by the experiment itself" (p. 146).
- **Artifact evaluation:** evaluating quality attributes of an artifact, usually by an AEC: reusable, functional, well-documented, consistent, complete (p. 146).
- **Badges (p. 146):** first badge at OOPSLA 2013 (Fig. 1). ACM's five badges in three groups: (a) available, without evaluation; (b) functional / reusable, from AE; (c) results reproduced (with the artifact) or replicated (without). Footnote 6: debated whether "Reproduced" can be earned inside AE or needs an independent study.
- **Terminology:** the paper uses the **2020 ACM terms** (reproduced = with the authors' artifact, replicated = without), matching the lecture. It does not discuss the terms further.
- **Venue** = conference + year (p. 147).

## Study method
- **Subjects (§3.2, p. 147):** SE and PL conferences with AE from Hermann et al.'s 2020 list, restricted to the **ACM Digital Library** for a common badge framework and uniform metadata.
  - 3 650 articles from 64 venues, 2011–2020: ASE, CGO, ESEC/FSE, ICFP, ICSE, ISSTA, MODELS, OOPSLA, PLDI, POPL, PPoPP, SLE (Fig. 3, p. 148).
  - Excluded: FSE 2012 (only a confidential best-artifact award) and MODELS 2019 (only workshops in the DL). ASE 2018 included without AE, because it gave Available badges.
  - Included: every article with an author, tagged "Research Article"/"Article", ≥ 4 pages (short tool papers count).
- **Four categories (§4.1, p. 148):** **AE** (artifact with Functional/Reusable or old venue badge), **Av.** (only Available badge), **NoB** (artifact, no badge), **NoA** (no artifact). For RQ2–5: AE vs. **NonAE** (= Av. + NoB, p. 149).
  - Old monochrome badges were detected from pixel colours in the PDF corners, then manually confirmed and cross-checked with conference sites, Conference Publishing Consulting, FindResearch and the AEC chairs.
  - 25 728 URLs from 3 150 PDFs were tagged manually with the tool **URLBrowser**.
- **RQ1 visibility:** Crossref citation counts. Two-sample **Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS)** tests per venue (to control for age), stratified by page length (cut-off 10 pages) and open/closed access, **Benjamini–Hochberg** correction. Topic (ACM CCS classes) dropped: too many classes with one article (pp. 148–149).
- **RQ2 availability (pp. 149–150):** three steps: a reference exists → it resolves → it is the artifact. Sources: ACM DL, Conference Publishing Consulting, FindResearch, the CMU dataset (Timperley et al.), article PDFs.
  - Resolving checked with cURL **HTTP HEAD** requests. On a sample: 2.5 % false positives, 5.6 % false negatives.
  - Step 3 not checked in depth: AEC's judgement trusted for AE; URLBrowser judgement for the rest.
  - 4 071 references classified into host platform types (Table 4, p. 151).
- **RQ3 maintenance (p. 152):** GitHub REST API for 1 056 repositories (535 AE, 521 NonAE): last commit, commits after publication, contributors, forks, stars/watchers → Dev. Time, Idle Time, Commit Density. KS tests, not corrected (two per measure).
- **RQ4 reuse (pp. 152–153):** artifact URLs found in the PDFs of **other articles in the same dataset**, split by whether author lists overlap.
- **RQ5 documentation (p. 153):** random sample of 100 AE + 100 NonAE artifacts, version from the AE notification date. 12 file-name patterns chosen "according to our experience" (readme, setup, install, doc/, examples/, assets/, artifact, report/supplement PDFs, copyright, licence) and their word counts.
- **Not checked:** whether any artifact runs or reproduces results; artifacts that failed AE (they sit in NonAE); causal effects (except RQ2).

## Findings
- **RQ1 (Tables 1–2, p. 149):** confounders matter in 14 of 64 venues (regular > short papers). After correction, significant differences in only **2 of 64 venues** (FSE 2014, ESEC/FSE 2019), and only NoB > NoA among short papers. Contradicts Heumüller et al. (small positive correlation, ICSE only, years without AE, p. 147).
- **RQ2 (Table 3, p. 150; Table 4, p. 151):**
  - AE + Available badge: 99.0 % have a reference, 99.9 % of those work.
  - NonAE without Available badge: 50.0 % have a reference, 89.9 % work.
  - No working reference: 0.4 % of Available-badge articles vs. 9.7 % without.
  - 8 of 754 Available-badge articles had no findable link in the paper or metadata (e.g. ICSE 2020's open review on GitHub).
  - Broken-link ratio by host: IP addresses and "other" 100 %, file storage 36 %, institutional websites 23 %, project websites 14 %, public VCS 4 %, publisher material 1 %, DOI/handle 1 %.
  - Since 2017 institutional and project links fall, DOI and publisher links rise (Fig. 4, p. 151).
- **RQ3 (Table 5, p. 152):** AE repositories have longer dev. time, higher commit density, shorter idle time, more contributors, stars and watchers. Effect sizes are small (D = 0.076–0.154). Authors read NonAE repositories as mainly archival.
- **RQ4 (Table 6, p. 153):** tiny numbers (69–138 articles, ≤ 0.11 %). More AE artifacts are referenced by unrelated authors, but the fewer NonAE artifacts get more referencing articles. "We cannot draw clear conclusions."
- **RQ5 (Table 7, p. 154):** 13 AE / 12 NonAE artifacts match no pattern. README in 84 AE / 86 NonAE, longer on average in AE. A few NonAE artifacts (15) have large doc/ or examples/ folders. Only 50 AE / 46 NonAE have a licence file. Of 26 AE artifacts without one, 22 had a licence only in ACM DL/Zenodo metadata.
  - Interpretation: AE documentation targets **reproduction**, some NonAE documentation targets **reuse** (link to Hermann's reviewer vs. user split).

## Threats (§9, pp. 154–155)
- **Construct:** AE participation is not public; badges are the proxy. Based on open-review venues, artifacts that benefited from AE without a badge are assumed negligible.
- **Internal:** associations only (except RQ2). How chairs run AE may matter strongly but is rarely documented. Stratification shrinks samples → results are "conservative". URL extraction is imperfect; URLs and tool are shared. Data errors found were reported to ACM and fixed.
- **External:** only ACM DL venues, but that is 64 of the 89 SE/PL AE data points in Hermann et al. (71.9 %).

## Recommendations (§10, p. 155)
- **Rewards:** badges don't raise citations, and artifacts escape citation tracking. Discuss alternatives: rigorous attribution policies in review guidelines, counts of evaluated artifacts, the **R+ index**, test-of-time awards for artifacts.
- **Available badge:** many NoB articles could easily get it. Link it to the **call for papers and paper review**, not to AE. Check camera-ready versions for the artifact link (Conference Publishing Consulting adopted this for ESEC/FSE 2022, footnote 18).
- **Research** what keeps authors from packaging and publishing.
- **Community standards** for documentation, packaging, submission, publication and referencing, extending ESEC/FSE's required files. Put licence files in the artifact, not just the metadata.
