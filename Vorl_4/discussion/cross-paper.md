# Cross-Paper Discussion: Broneske et al. (2024) × Guevara-Vega et al. (2024)

## Quick comparison

| | Broneske et al. | Guevara-Vega et al. |
|---|---|---|
| Kind of artifact | Datasets from **other people's** version-control data | Material from experiments **with human participants** |
| Sample | 41 datasets (MSR 2017–2021) + papers using them, 200 papers | 267 journal HOE studies (2000–2022), 106 public artifacts; 12 awarded conference artifacts |
| Method | Qualitative coding, one coder; **one** expert interview | Survey by one screener; design science proposal; self-validation |
| Main problem found | Ethics, consent, licences; reuse fails on technical issues | Artifacts lack design, protocol, structure, DOI |
| Output | Three recommendations | A structured outline mapped to ACM badges |
| Availability finding | 3 of 6 website-hosted datasets offline | 22 % broken links among studies with an artifact |
| Own artifact | Zenodo dataset | Zenodo artifact, but "data on request" |
| Terminology | No definitions | ACM 2020 badge meanings |

## Discussion questions

1. **Two kinds of "people in the data".** Broneske: developers who never agreed to be
   studied. Guevara-Vega: participants who did consent, but only to a specific study. Which
   is harder to share responsibly, and should the rules differ?
2. **Rules vs. structure.** Broneske gives principles (minimise, check licences),
   Guevara-Vega a detailed template. Which would change your own practice more?
3. **Badges as the yardstick.** Guevara-Vega optimises artifacts for ACM badges; Broneske
   barely mentions badges. Do badges help with ethical and legal questions at all?
4. **Thin validation in both.** One expert; self-validation on own experiments. What
   would convincing validation of a recommendation or a template look like?
5. **Context over data.** Both find the data itself is usually there; what's missing is
   context (how it was collected, licences, design, protocol). Is "artifact" the wrong
   unit, and should papers carry more of it?
6. **Links vs. snapshots.** Broneske suggests sharing links plus a method; Guevara-Vega
   requires a DOI and a persistent repository for Available. Which serves replicability
   better for each kind of data?

## Broader / subjective discussion

- **Outside the box:** if a developer asks to be removed from a published dataset that
  dozens of papers built on, what should happen to those results?
- Should repository mining and human experiments go through the same ethics review?
- After four weeks of papers: is the main barrier to good artifacts now knowledge
  (what to share), effort (time), or permission (privacy, licences)?
- One concrete thing you would do differently in your own next dataset or experiment.
