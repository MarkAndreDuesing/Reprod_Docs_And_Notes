# Guevara-Vega, Bernárdez, Cruz, Durán, Ruiz-Cortés & Solari (2024): Condensed Notes

_Page numbers are PDF pages (1–16)._

## Core claims
- Open science in SE is done non-systematically; artifacts are often incomplete (p. 1).
- Human-oriented experiments (HOEs) need richer artifacts than computational experiments
  (participant instructions, surveys, training material) (pp. 1–2).
- Only **39.7 %** of 267 journal HOE studies (2000–2022) have fully accessible artifacts
  (p. 6).
- A structured artifact outline, mapped to ACM badges and Solari's maturity levels, helps
  authors reach badges (§5).

## Key definitions
- **Research artifact:** ACM's "digital object created by the authors to be used as part
  of a study or generated by the experiment" (p. 2); older names: laboratory package,
  replication package, experimental material (p. 4).
- **ACM badges (p. 3–4):** Available (repository with DOI), Evaluated (Functional,
  Reusable), Validated (Reproduced = independent study using part of the original setup;
  Replicated = independent study with its own setup). These match the lecture's **2020**
  terms.
- **Solari & Vegas maturity levels (p. 4):** Article → Operational → Descriptive →
  Procedural → Decision-making.
- **Solari's seven modules (p. 5):** Introduction, Theory, Training, Experiment,
  Evolution, Replication, Aggregation.

## Study method
- **Design science research** in four phases: literature, survey, proposal, validation
  (Fig. 1, p. 3).
- **Survey:** Web of Science query "experiment" in title/abstract, EMSE/IST/JSS/TSE, since
  2000 → 1426 → 267 HOE studies → 106 public artifacts (Fig. 2, pp. 5–6). Screening mainly
  by **one researcher**.
- **Proposal:** iterative, based on ACM guidelines, Solari's proposals and the survey.
- **Validation:** 12 artifacts from ASE, ICSE, FSE and RE artifact tracks (not in the survey),
  plus 2 of the authors' own experiments (pp. 9–12).

## Findings
- **Availability (p. 6):** of 147 studies with an artifact, 8 need authorisation, 33 have
  broken links (22.4 %), 106 accessible.
- **Trend (p. 6):** about 5 artifacts per year, jump to 35 in 2020.
- **Guidelines (Table 2, p. 8):** journal guidelines are very general; only EMSE has badges.
- **Repositories (p. 8):** GitHub 31 %, institutional 28 %, Zenodo 16 %, Figshare 6 %.
- **Top-ten problems (Table 3, p. 8):** design description missing (91.5 %), protocol
  missing (88.8 %), no structure (75.5 %), no DOI (62.3 %), material missing (59.4 %) …
  datasets missing only 6.6 %.
- **Modules (Fig. 6, p. 9):** all have the Experiment module but only 35.9 % complete;
  Training 49 %; only one artifact (Basili et al.) covers all modules.
- **Validation (Table 7, p. 12):** mean coverage 85.6 % (Available), 85.7 % (Reusable);
  unawarded artifacts lack DOI and instructions.

## Proposed structure (Table 4, p. 10)
- Sections: Article · Experimental material · Datasets · System · Scripts · Research
  artifact dictionary · (optional) Reproduction/Replication information.
- **Mandatory for Available:** raw data, script description, analysis scripts, artifact
  description, DOI, public repository (p. 9).
- **Mandatory for Reusable:** raw data, script description, analysis scripts, inventory,
  description, accessible data, download and execution guidelines (p. 9).

## Critique (tightened)
- Validation is partly circular (items derived from and checked against awarded
  artifacts) and partly self-validation (own experiments; acknowledged, p. 13).
- Odd numbers: "55.5 % of 6 items" (p. 9); decimal commas mixed with points.
- Own data availability "on request" (p. 14) vs. Zenodo artifact (p. 13).
- Counterpoint: concrete, checklist-like output that authors can use tomorrow; mapping to
  badges makes it directly actionable.

## Discussion points (merged)
- Should the badge criteria themselves be made this concrete, or should venues stay flexible?
- Is a 40-item outline realistic for a PhD student's first experiment?
- Design and protocol are "missing" from 90 % of artifacts but usually in the paper itself:
  is that really a defect?
